Fine-tuning works, but it does not catch the 2D model

Fine-tuned Nesso is substantially better than unchanged Nesso across nested development, the historical check set, and the public challenge data. The established 2D model remains more accurate and ranks compounds better in all three comparisons.

Conclusion

Fine-tuning Nesso answers the transfer-learning question positively. It does not establish competitive performance against the conventional 2D model for this PXR endpoint.

Retrospective challenge analysis

The 513 challenge labels were public before this analysis. Challenge results are retrospective external-dataset evidence, not a temporal blind test.

Where performance differs

  • Assay uncertainty: 2D is better in every assay-error quartile; in the highest-error quartile its nested MAE is 0.788 versus 1.087 for Nesso.
  • Charge: among negatively charged compounds, nested MAE is 0.551 for 2D versus 0.872 for Nesso.
  • Similarity: 2D is better in every nearest-training-similarity band.
  • Middle potency: Nesso has a small MAE advantage for observed pEC50 between 4 and 5 in nested development (0.331 versus 0.349) and the challenge set (0.296 versus 0.377).
  • Lower Emax: the models are nearly tied on MAE across 272 additionally curated lower-Emax development compounds: 0.533 for Nesso and 0.531 for 2D.

Active-tail and calibration limits

Shared failure

Both learned models compress the highly active tail. Neither predicts any of the eight shared-check or 31 challenge compounds above pEC50 6. In nested development, Nesso predicts one of 55 above 6 and 2D predicts none.

Fine-tuning improves aggregate performance while making active-tail error worse than unchanged Nesso on the check and challenge sets. The nested calibration slope is 1.067 for Nesso and 1.011 for 2D; on the public challenge it is 1.411 for Nesso and 1.055 for 2D, showing stronger range compression in Nesso.

Interpretation limits

  • The legacy label-free 2D variance/correlation prefilter was fixed from development structures rather than recalculated in every new outer fold.
  • The historical check set has already been opened and cannot support continued iterative tuning as a pristine holdout.
  • The public challenge analysis is retrospective; truth was available before this comparison.
  • The challenge feature extraction has strong internal parity, but a discrepancy with a separate reference run remains to resolve before publication.
  • Potency, charge, similarity, active-tail, and activity-cliff subgroups are exploratory rather than multiplicity-controlled confirmatory tests.
