Model design

How the model was trained

Nesso-1 does most of the molecular work. It turns each PXR–molecule pair into two sets of 384 numbers. We trained only the two small parts that turn those numbers into a PXR activity prediction.

What happens for each molecule

  1. Nesso receives the prepared molecule and the PXR protein sequence.
  2. Nesso describes the pair with two sets of 384 numbers.
  3. Those summaries are saved so they do not have to be rebuilt during every training epoch.
  4. Each set goes through its own small prediction model.
  5. The final prediction is the average of the two answers.

What was trained

Only the two small prediction models learned from the PXR measurements. The rest of Nesso-1 stays fixed. This keeps training short enough to repeat across five structure-based groups and three starting points.

Training settings

Stop ruleStop after five epochs without a useful drop in error
Starting points42, 43, and 44

How the model was chosen

Each setting was tested across the five development groups. The main score is the average size of the prediction error in pEC50 units. When two settings were close, we preferred the one that did a better job of ordering compounds from weaker to stronger. The selected setting showed the same pattern in all three repeat runs.

The PXR challenge compounds did not affect the training settings. The chosen setup was checked once on the 790-compound set and then trained on all 4,134 labeled compounds. Challenge predictions are the next step.
