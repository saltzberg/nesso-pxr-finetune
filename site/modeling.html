Matched model design

Two models evaluated on the same chemical groups

Fine-tuned Nesso-1 and a conventional 2D LightGBM model use different molecular representations, but their development estimates come from the same 3,344 compounds and the same five outer chemical-family folds.

Fine-tuned Nesso-1

  1. Nesso receives the prepared molecule and the PXR protein sequence.
  2. It produces two 384-number representations of the protein–molecule pair.
  3. Those representations are cached so the expensive structure model does not run during every training epoch.
  4. Each representation passes through its own released Nesso regression head.
  5. Only those two heads are updated; the final pEC50 prediction is their average.

Nested development estimate

For each outer chemical group, all model choices are made using only the other four groups. The original bounded Nesso settings are compared inside that outer-training partition. The median inner best epoch fixes training length, then three heads are refit on all outer-training compounds. Selected lengths range from 26 to 28 epochs. The untouched outer group supplies the reported prediction.

The historical challenge model is related but distinct: its settings were frozen after the original development screen, three heads were trained on all 4,134 labels, and their predictions were averaged. Challenge outcomes did not fit those heads.

2D LightGBM comparator

The 2D model uses physicochemical descriptors and structural fingerprints calculated directly from the molecular graph. Inputs include molecular weight and lipophilicity, VSA and BCUT descriptors, atom-pair, Morgan/ECFP, layered, pattern, Avalon, ErG, pharmacophore, topological, CATS2D, scaffold-key, EState, MACCS, and functional-group features.

Supervised 2D feature selection and stopping are nested. The earlier label-free variance/correlation prefilter remains fixed rather than being recalculated inside every outer fold, so this is a nested supervised estimate with fixed legacy preprocessing.
