Model design

How the model will be trained

Nesso-1 does most of the molecular work. It turns each PXR–molecule pair into two sets of 384 numbers. We will train only the two small parts that turn those numbers into a PXR activity prediction.

What happens for each molecule

  1. Nesso receives the prepared molecule and the PXR protein sequence.
  2. Nesso describes the pair with two sets of 384 numbers.
  3. Those summaries are saved so they do not have to be rebuilt during every training epoch.
  4. Each set goes through its own small prediction model.
  5. The final prediction is the average of the two answers.

What will change

Only the two small prediction models will learn from the PXR measurements. The rest of Nesso-1 stays fixed. This keeps training short enough to repeat across five structure-based groups and three starting points.

How a model will be chosen

Each setting is tested across the five development groups. The main score is the average size of the prediction error in pEC50 units. If two settings are close, we will prefer the one that does a better job of ordering compounds from weaker to stronger. The chosen setting must show the same pattern in at least two of the three repeat runs.

The PXR challenge compounds do not affect the training settings. After one check on the 790-compound set, the chosen setup will be trained on all 4,134 labeled compounds. Challenge predictions will be created only after that model is fixed.
